\section{Extensions}

The same pattern holds across all extensions: messages that rarely and predictably change the receiver's answer are certified (the Llama-8B receiver, Text+fact nominally, SQuAD, and sampled answers, on OBQA nominally). A stronger helper for the medium receiver falls back because its calibration splits are too small, while the remaining settings fall back (the OLMo and Llama pairs, GSM8K). Each ran under a protocol fixed before its outputs (Appendix~\ref{app:medium}).

\subsection{Cross-family receivers and helpers}\label{app:xfam}

Each cross-family pair ran the main study's protocol, using prompts in each model's native chat template, under its own frozen protocol, with every setting reported. The OLMo pair combines the large helper (whose saved messages serve as Text messages, matching token counts on every row) with an OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct receiver \citep{olmo2025olmo2}, for which no C2C fuser exists (a 60-test family). The Llama pair combines a Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct helper \citep{grattafiori2024llama3} with the small receiver on OBQA and ARC (an 80-test family). Its C2C outputs come from the official evaluator and cross-family fuser run on our rows, with receiver prompts byte-identical to ours across all 5,626 rows and 16 saved small-pair C2C outputs reproduced bit for bit. All seven settings fall back (Table~\ref{tab:dev_full}). OLMo disagrees with Text on 23.2, 22.4, and 42.1\% of OBQA, ARC, and MMLU-Pro development questions (Qwen3-8B: 9.7, 5.0, and 21.2\%), at AUROC 0.790, 0.749, and 0.633 (0.873, 0.952, and 0.821), and answers 71.6, 68.2, and 22.4\% correctly alone. The Llama helper raises the small receiver's accuracy from 38.4 to 44.9 (Text) and 48.2\% (C2C) on OBQA, and from 36.8 to 48.5 and 48.8\% on ARC. Its Text messages change fewer answers than the Qwen helper's (25.7 and 29.4\% against 45.7 and 48.5\%), but they are ranked weakly (AUROC 0.693 and 0.678), and the fuser changes 57.5 and 58.2\%.

Part of the OLMo fallback stems from answer format, as unparseable outputs count as changes: 4.3, 9.2, and 17.1\% of OLMo's receiver-only and 5.9, 8.0, and 13.1\% of its Text calibration answers are \textsc{invalid} (on development 2.8, 12.0, and 17.0\% and 3.5, 6.4, and 13.7\%; Llama-pair receiver 3.4 and 10.7\%, references 2.7 to 6.9\%; Llama-8B pair at most 0.1\%). Furthermore, 72 of 320, 47 of 95, and 968 of 2,457 calibration disagreements involve exactly one \textsc{invalid} output. A non-deployable, post-hoc filter retaining only questions where both answers parse certifies all three OLMo settings ($q=0.45$, 0.40, and 0.05; development disagreement 18.8, 15.5, and 33.5\%, AUROC 0.855, 0.881, and 0.701) but no Llama setting. No deployable rule we tested certifies OLMo or any Llama setting. Post hoc, answering skipped questions with the probe's argmax agrees with OLMo's own answer on 95.2, 90.2, and 81.1\% of calibration questions but passes no threshold (smallest $p=0.901$, 0.377, and 1.000; AUROC 0.808, 0.765, and 0.653), because the reference's unparseable answers (15.8, 29.2, and 31.2\% of development disagreements) remain changes; routing unparseable receiver answers to the reference also fails (Appendix~\ref{app:restson}).

\paragraph{Llama-8B pair.} A Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct receiver reading the large helper's saved messages was added after these fell back, as the first of three candidates in an order fixed before any output (followed by Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 and Granite-3.1-8B-Instruct, which produced no outputs, making OLMo-2 the only other non-Qwen receiver run). It used its default chat template and a 40-test family. It disagrees with Text on 11.6 and 12.4\% of OBQA and ARC development questions (AUROC 0.874 and 0.880) and is certified at $q=0.60$ (21 of 800 skipped calibration answers change, $p=5.7\times10^{-4}$) and $q=0.70$ (4 of 320, $p=3.2\times10^{-4}$). On development, 7 of 438 (1.60\% [0.64, 3.27]) and 4 of 206 (1.94\% [0.53, 4.90]) skipped answers change. Both deploy in all 200 re-drawn splits and keep their quantile on questions whose two answers parse and with argmax answers; no score is exactly zero. Text raises accuracy from 603 to 636 of 742 and from 239 to 260 of 299, with the policies answering 638 and 258 correctly.

\subsection{Informed and stronger helpers}\label{app:informed}

\paragraph{Text+fact.} Post hoc, the large helper's input gains one line before the question, \texttt{Useful fact: <fact1>}, providing the OpenBookQA science fact behind the question. Consequently, the receiver sees the fact only through the message. Receiver-only outputs, scores, and thresholds are reused in a separate 20-test family. The message changes 12.4\% of calibration answers (170 of 1,366; plain Text 10.5\%) and 10.2\% of development answers (AUROC 0.889). The test deploys $q=0.75$ (22 of 999, $p=4.8\times10^{-6}$; $q=0.80$ fails, 33 of 1,064, $p=0.0016$), also without the 208 reviewed questions (20 of 852). The certificate is nominal because ProbeMax was chosen on the plain Text labels of the same questions; held out, 16 of 558 skipped answers change ($p=9.2\times10^{-3}$). On development, the policy skips 536 of 742 questions and changes 13. The message corrects 62 development answers and breaks 9; the policy skips 11 of the corrections and 1 of the breaks, answering 660 of 742 correctly against 670 for Text+fact and 617 for $R$ ($-1.35$ points [$-2.29$, $-0.54$], within $\kappa\alpha\approx3.6$). This is the only deployed policy whose development accuracy interval lies below zero.

\paragraph{A stronger helper.} Under a protocol fixed before its outputs, the large helper's stored messages were given to the medium receiver on OBQA and ARC (40 tests). The helper alone answers 86.3 and 87.6\% of development questions correctly (receiver: 66.0 and 72.6\%). Its messages raise the receiver's accuracy by 7.3 and 4.3 points but change 21.2 and 13.4\% of its development answers (calibration: 297 of 1,366 and 55 of 448; AUROC 0.792 and 0.800). Both settings fall back (smallest $p=0.26$ and 0.026); they certify in none and 15.5\% of 200 re-drawn splits, and would need about 21,500 and 880 calibration questions to pass.

\paragraph{Design and selection.} A pilot protocol fixed before any pilot output defined eight candidates (SQuAD and NQ with passages, TriviaQA and NQ-Open closed-book \citep{rajpurkar2016squad,kwiatkowski2019nq,fisch2019mrqa,joshi2017triviaqa,lee2019latent}, each with a Qwen3-8B and a Llama-8B receiver and the large helper), three scores, and a label-free selection rule. A full-run protocol fixed before any full-run output added a 1,000-question test split and narrowed the reporting rule, so that an unqualified claim for extractive questions required a passing test as well as a certificate (recorded deviations from the pilot protocol); the certificate was fixed before any development or test output existed. For SQuAD, the training pool is deduplicated and limited to passages of at most 300 words (86,830 questions), and all splits are disjoint blocks of a fixed pseudorandom order. The receiver answers directly with at most 32 new tokens, and answers are compared using official SQuAD normalization. The three scores are one minus the top probability at the first generated token containing a letter or digit (analogous to ProbeMax), one minus the whole answer's probability, and one minus its per-token geometric mean; the whole-answer score had the best pilot AUROC in all eight settings. It needs the receiver-only answer, which is served when the message is skipped and wasted otherwise. A setting was eligible if at most 4\% of its 25\% lowest-score pilot questions changed; eligible settings were ranked by the certification probability $P$ that the frozen binormal model (Appendix~\ref{app:predict}) predicts from the pilot's disagreement rate and AUROC, and at most two with $P\ge0.5$ would run, each at the smallest calibration size with $P\ge0.8$. The four Qwen3-8B settings and NQ-Open with the Llama-8B receiver are ineligible (7 to 30 of 100 changed), and the rule selected SQuAD with the Llama-8B receiver at 2,000 calibration questions ($P=0.975$, inflated by taking the best of eight; conservative 0.081) and no second setting (next: 0.413 and 0.393; Table~\ref{tab:squad_pilot}).

\paragraph{Results.} Before production, the runtime reproduced 16 pilot questions exactly. On calibration, the receiver disagrees with Text on 302 of 2,000 questions (15.1\%), and the test deploys $q=0.35$ (17 of 721, $p=2.5\times10^{-4}$; Table~\ref{tab:squad_cal}). All 200 re-drawn splits certify (median $q=0.375$, median development coverage 41.5\%). On development (disagreement 15.4\% [13.2, 17.8], AUROC 0.811 [0.771, 0.848]), the policy skips 39.6\% of questions, and 16 of 396 skipped answers change (4.04\% [2.3, 6.5]). In 15 of these, one normalized answer contains the other; counting containment as agreement leaves 1. On the sealed test, 9 of 364 skipped answers change (2.47\% [1.14, 4.64], $p=0.012$); the pass rule is a two-sided 95\% upper bound below 5\%, so the test passes, though $p$ exceeds 0.001. On development and test, $R$ answers 659 and 664 of 1,000 questions correctly, Text 669 and 682, and the policy 673 and 686. Among the skipped changes, Text would have corrected 4 and 2 answers and broken 8 and 6. Text's development gain, 1.0 point [$-0.9$, $+2.9$], is below $\kappa\alpha=2.0$, and its interval includes zero, so the protocol's redundancy rule holds. From recorded per-question times (sequential execution, on a different machine than the \S\ref{sec:rq3} replays), skipping saves an estimated 198.5~ms [167.2, 229.9] per question, while always skipping saves 785.4~ms (helper 772.5~ms; receiver 153.5~ms alone and 166.4~ms with the message, since the receiver-only answer is always computed). This is an estimate, not a replay.

\subsection{A numeric-answer check: GSM8K}\label{app:gsm}

A prespecified check (large pair, Text) used 400 fit, 1,000 calibration, and 400 development training questions in a fixed content-based order (a recorded deviation from the main protocol's seeded shuffle) and raised the receiver's limit from 64 to 320 tokens (a deviation fixed in advance), because the receiver derives before answering. A change is a mismatch of the normalized final number (after the last ``answer is'', otherwise the last number; the extractor was tested on twelve hand-written strings and, as prespecified, never changed). The score is one minus the length-normalized probability of the receiver's answer tokens under teacher forcing, which reduces to ProbeMax for a single label token. No candidate passes (every $p=1.000$), although all 3,200 outputs parse and the receiver disagrees with Text on 17.4\% of calibration and 19.3\% of development questions (accuracy 87.75\% alone, 86.50\% with Text). The score fails: 378 of 400 fit questions score exactly zero after the derivation, so 18 of the 20 thresholds coincide. Its development AUROC is 0.5795 (0.6444 for the well-spread whole-continuation probability, 0.5680 for the first answer token). The last-number rule fired on 15.2\% of receiver-only outputs, tracking the 320-token cap (reached by 43 fit, 106 calibration, and 49 development outputs); the Text path is barely affected (6 of 1,000 and 2 of 400). A later protocol, written after this fallback, planned a score over the first 32 generated tokens but stopped at its reproduction check (differences up to 0.04 and 0.06 on two capped outputs, against a tolerance of $10^{-4}$), so no prefix score was tested (Table~\ref{tab:provenance}).

\subsection{Sampled receiver answers}\label{app:sampling}

Post hoc, under a protocol fixed before its outputs, the large pair's OBQA and ARC Text settings were rerun with receiver answers sampled on both paths (the Qwen3 model card's settings with thinking disabled: temperature 0.7, top-$p$ 0.8, top-$k$ 20, min-$p$ 0; one sample per question and path, seeded independently), with messages, scores, thresholds, and the test unchanged in a 40-test family (OBQA stays nominal). Both settings deploy at the greedy thresholds ($q=0.80$: 19 of 1,064, $p=3.4\times10^{-8}$; $q=0.95$: 4 of 422, $p=4.8\times10^{-6}$) and in all 200 re-drawn splits, and 3.32\% [2.01, 5.14] and 2.82\% [1.22, 5.47] of skipped development answers change. Sampled disagreement is 9.4 and 4.7\% (AUROC 0.867 and 0.956; greedy: 9.7 and 5.0\%), most skipped questions still have zero uncertainty (354 of 572 and 217 of 284), and accuracy is 617 and 646 of 742 (receiver alone, Text) and 268 and 275 of 299. Sampling changes few of this receiver's labels (6 of 742 receiver-only and 2 Text answers on OBQA, 0 and 1 of 299 on ARC), so this check says nothing about receivers whose samples vary more or that generate reasoning tokens.
